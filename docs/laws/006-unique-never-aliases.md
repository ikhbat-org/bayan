## Statement
Outside `unsafe` code, every program in the table gets its listed verdict. Rejected, each with its ownership diagnostic: a use of a moved local on any path (straight-line, in a loop body on a later iteration, after an `if` or `switch` where one branch moved it, at scope exit through a `defer` or, on a failing exit, an `errdefer`); an implicit copy of a `unique` type or of any type containing a `unique` part; a borrow (`ref`, span, slice, borrowed loop or pattern binding) stored somewhere that outlives its owner; a move out of a field, element or plain parameter; `take()` on a type without an empty value or on a place that can't be written; `switch move x` on a subject that is not a local or `move` parameter, followed by a use of `x`, with a pattern that binds a value and a part of it, or with a `when` guard that mutates or moves one of its arm's bindings. Accepted: the same programs written with an explicit `.copy(to: alloc)` on a type that has one (`List`, `Map`, `StrBuf`; HQ68, round-4 batch 5), with `take()` in place of a move out of a field or element, with `switch move x` in place of moving a pattern binding of a borrowed subject (its bindings disjoint), with the moved local reassigned before the use, and with the borrow ending before its owner does.

## Counterexample looks like
A program that compiles where `var b = move a;` is followed on some path by `a.append(1)`; where `var c = ledger;` copies a struct holding a `List`; where `let items = move ledger.items;` leaves `ledger` half-moved; or a rejection of `var b = move a; a = List<i64>(); a.append(1);`.

## Proof notes
`tests/Bayan.Tests/L006OwnershipGuardTests.cs`, tagged `[Trait("Law", "L006")]`, compiles a table through `Compilation.Create` with a shared prelude. 43 rows must be rejected with their ownership diagnostic: moved-local uses on every listed path, implicit copies of unique types and types containing them, escaping borrows, moves out of a field, element or plain parameter, a moved pattern binding of a borrowed subject (BYN2783), a use of `x` after `switch move x`, of a binding after it was moved on in the same arm, and of `switch move s` on a later loop iteration when `s` is declared outside the loop (BYN2781), `switch move …` on a field or plain parameter (BYN2789), an owning pattern that binds both sides of an `and` or a designation and a part inside it (BYN2790), a guard of `switch move …` that mutates its binding (BYN2791), and `take()` on a `let` local or plain parameter (BYN2104) or on `i64`/`string` (BYN2788). 26 rows must be accepted: explicit `.copy(to:)`, reassignment before use, borrows that end before their owner, `take()` from a `List` field, list element, `Map` element, optional field, `ref` parameter and `StrBuf` local (a `StrBuf` cannot be a struct field, BYN3001), and `switch move x` in statement and expression form and on a `move` parameter, with the bindings moved on, with two disjoint bindings of one variant both moved on, with two disjoint bindings reached through one field written twice, and with a binding next to an `i64` copy of its length. Amended 2026-10-21 (spec 003) to add the `take()` and `switch move x` rows; a break making owning bindings borrow fails 4 rows, and silencing BYN2789 fails 2. The review of spec 003 added the BYN2790 rows; silencing BYN2790 fails both and its golden. The `.copy(to:)` rows cover `List`, `Map` and `StrBuf`, the types that have one (spec §9.3: "when the type has one"); a struct, union, tuple or optional holding a unique part has no `.copy`, so it has no accepted copy row (Q68).
